## 1. Andisols / volcanic-ash soils (Colombian Andes, coffee zone, páramo)

### Takeaway
Andisols hold exceptionally large amounts of water because of short-range-order minerals (allophane, imogolite) and, in non-allophanic and páramo soils, Al/Fe–humus complexes. Their bulk density is very low (<0.9 Mg m⁻³). Drying is irreversible: air-drying can cut water retention by up to ~40%, and desiccation can shrink void space by ~50%. As a result, the standard lab practice of measuring retention at −33/−1500 kPa on air-dried, sieved samples (still used in some Colombian studies) systematically underestimates FC. Texture-based PTFs are also poorly suited to these soils, since organic matter and amorphous materials, not clay content, drive retention.

### Cited Findings
**Mechanisms of high retention**
- Buytaert et al. (2006), "Human impact on the hydrology of the Andean páramos", *Earth-Science Reviews* 79(1–2):53–72, state that the extremely high water retention of Andosols "is mostly attributed to the presence of amorphous clay minerals such as allophane and imogolite", and that organic matter also plays a role in non-allophanic soils — [Buytaert et al. 2006, open PDF (CONDESAN)](https://condesan.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Articulo2.pdf); [alt. copy](https://paramo.cc.ic.ac.uk/pubs/2006_ESR.pdf)
- A *Geoderma* study of non-allophanic Andisols with hydric properties from the Ecuadorian páramos (commonly cited as Poulenard et al. 2003; authorship not shown in the snippet) found that "in non-allophanic soils, organic colloids are the constituents responsible for their large porosity". It also notes that Hydric Andisols "are known to experience important irreversible physical changes on drying", which "makes them very fragile" — [ScienceDirect, Geoderma](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016706103001289)
- A Spanish-language Andosol summary attributes the "extraordinary porosity" created by allophane's structure to its huge water retention, and describes very high −1500 kPa retention in Andean páramo Andosols, indicating that micropores dominate — [Buytaert et al., "Hidrología del páramo andino" (Spanish)](https://paramo.cc.ic.ac.uk/pubs/ES/Hidroparamo2.pdf); [Univ. Extremadura FAO Andosol notes](https://www.eweb.unex.es/eweb/edafo/FAO/Andosol.htm)

**Irreversible drying effects (directly relevant to FC measurement)**
- Buytaert et al. (2006): "Drying these soils induces structural changes and hydrophobicity in the organic matter. The result is an irreversible decrease of the water retention, up to 40%" — [Buytaert et al. 2006](https://condesan.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Articulo2.pdf)
- A *Soil Use and Management* (2002) 18:94–100 paper on Umbric Andosols from south Ecuador reports a **40% reduction in water retention due to air drying in the laboratory**. It concludes that organic matter content "appears to be very important and certainly more important than allophane clay content" in controlling retention. It links the loss to destruction of the typical form of allophane particles by air-drying. Title and authorship are inferred: it is very likely Buytaert et al. 2002, "Impact of land use changes on the hydrological properties of volcanic ash soils in South Ecuador". Confirm. — [SUM 2002 PDF](https://paramo.cc.ic.ac.uk/pubs/SUM.pdf); [title listing on academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/62581590/Impact_of_land_use_changes_on_the_hydrological_properties_of_volcanic_ash_soils_in_South_Ecuador)
- Dorel et al. (2000), "Porosity and soil water properties of Caribbean volcanic ash soils", *Soil Use and Management* 16:133–140 (Guadeloupe Andosols vs. Nitisols): desiccation from 1 kPa to 1550 kPa causes significant shrinkage in the Andosol, a **50% reduction of void space**. The authors attribute this to microaggregation of non-crystalline components on drying. The halloysite/Fe-oxide Nitisol shrank little. Mechanized banana plots had clods with much less macroporosity, and compaction was a problem near field capacity — [Wiley abstract, DOI 10.1111/j.1475-2743.2000.tb00188.x](https://bsssjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1475-2743.2000.tb00188.x)
- A CIRAD (French) thesis summarizing Dorel et al. (2000) says they built a linear model that predicts macroporosity at low water content from allophane and organic-matter content alone. This is effectively a mineralogy-based PTF rather than a texture-based one — [CIRAD/UCLouvain thesis](https://agritrop.cirad.fr/564041/1/document_564041.pdf)
- An Indonesian Andisol study reports that Andisols commonly show irreversible characters after drying. Rewetted samples held less clay and water than at initial field capacity — [ResearchGate, drying–rewetting of Andisols](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/307736263_The_changes_of_soil_physical_and_chemical_properties_of_Andisols_as_affected_by_drying_and_rewetting_processes)
- A French-language *Volcanic Soils* chapter (Fernández Caldas & Yaalon, eds.) uses the rate of dehydration irreversibility after air-drying as a diagnostic criterion — [IRD Horizon PDF](https://horizon.documentation.ird.fr/exl-doc/pleins_textes/pleins_textes_5/b_fdi_23-25/29189.pdf)
- Humboldt University tropical-soils notes warn that excessive air-drying of Andosol material irreversibly deteriorates water-holding properties — [HU Berlin, Andosols](https://www.bodenkunde-projekte.hu-berlin.de/tropics/pcboku10.agrar.hu-berlin.de/cocoon/tropen/andosols1315.html?section=4)
- The WRB hydric criterion requires ≥100% water retention at 1500 kPa **on undried samples**. Measurements on air-dried material can therefore differ greatly from the classification criterion — [Univ. Extremadura FAO Andosol notes](https://www.eweb.unex.es/eweb/edafo/FAO/Andosol.htm)

**Bulk density and Colombian diagnostic criteria**
- Andosol bulk density is typically <0.9 Mg m⁻³, and not only in the topsoil. One source says bulk density changes little over the 0–1500 kPa range (limited swelling and shrinkage). That claim conflicts with Dorel et al.'s 50% void-space loss on drying, so treat it with caution — [Univ. Extremadura FAO Andosol notes](https://www.eweb.unex.es/eweb/edafo/FAO/Andosol.htm); contrast [Dorel et al. 2000](https://bsssjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1475-2743.2000.tb00188.x)
- Andic diagnostic properties used in Colombia for fine earth: bulk density at 33 kPa moisture <0.9 Mg m⁻³, phosphate retention ≥85%, and Alo + ½Feo ≥2%. These are cited in a paper on Andisols of eastern Antioquia (*Rev. Fac. Nal. Agr. Medellín* 62(1)) — [RFNAM 62(1), SciELO Colombia PDF](http://www.scielo.org.co/pdf/rfnam/v62n1/a17v62n1.pdf)

**Colombian and Andean data**
- An Andisol study on forest and other land uses (Redalyc, structure-stability paper, Colombian) measured retention on **air-dried samples** with pressure membranes at 33 kPa (FC) and 1500 kPa (PWP). Water retention at 1500 kPa ranged from 11 to 23% (gravimetric), highest in forest surface horizons (mean 20.6%). This illustrates the use of air-dried protocols in Colombian practice, which per the findings above underestimate field-moist retention — [Redalyc 57311108002](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/573/57311108002.pdf)
- Cenicafé measures FC as water retained at 0.33 bar and PWP at 15 bar, using pressure pots and a pressure membrane. Lince-Salazar & Sadeghian (2019), *Revista Cenicafé* 70(1):65–80, studied the spatial variability of physical properties (including FC and PWP) on two volcanic-ash lots in the coffee zone. They concluded that grid sampling was not the best design for geostatistics on heterogeneous slopes — [Cenicafé, "Variabilidad espacial de las propiedades físicas de andisoles en la región cafetera colombiana"](https://publicaciones.cenicafe.org/index.php/cenicafe/article/view/3287)
- "Propiedades hidrofísicas de suelos de la zona cafetera colombiana y su relación con el material parental" (RIAA/UNAD) reports gravimetric FC across coffee-zone soils ranging from **27.1 ± 1.5% to 53.83 ± 5.3%**, with the lowest PWP at 19.0 ± 1.8%. Volcanic-ash soils had the highest water retention — [UNAD RIAA](https://hemeroteca.unad.edu.co/index.php/riaa/article/view/5891); [AmeliCA full text](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/130/1303815003/html/); [Dialnet PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/8719481.pdf)
- "Dinámica del agua en andisoles bajo condiciones de ladera" (*Rev. Fac. Nal. Agr. - A Cenicafé/coffee-zone soil-taxonomy document describes Andisol physical properties as good because of high organic matter and a favourable sand/clay balance, making them less susceptible to water loss in dry spells — [ResearchGate, "Taxonomía de suelos. Consideraciones para la zona cafetera"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355950792_Taxonomia_de_suelos_Consideraciones_para_la_zona_cafetera_de_Colombia)
- "Soil moisture retention and mass movement of volcanic soils from the 'Sabinas' sector in Caldas, Colombia" (*Acta Agronómica* 66(4)) exists, but its content could not be read — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/1699/169952658020/html/)
- Colombian páramo study (Nariño): PTFs fitted to the available data were used to estimate bulk density for SOC stocks, not water retention — [Terra Latinoamericana / SciELO MX](https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?pid=S0187-57792018000200183&script=sci_arttext_plus&tlng=es)
- Mexican Andosol study (*Rev. Mex. Ciencias Forestales*): six PTFs for FC/PWP were validated, and land-use change from forest to crops altered Andosol water retention. This is the closest Latin American Andosol PTF analogue found — [INIFAP Ciencias Forestales](https://cienciasforestales.inifap.gob.mx/index.php/forestales/article/view/666)

**Land-use effects (hydrology)**
- Land-use change in Ecuadorian páramo catchments causes irreversible structural changes that reduce water retention and severely reduce both peak and low flows — [Catena 2021, "Influence of land use on hydro-physical soil properties of Andean páramos"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0341816221000862)
- A tropical Andisol study tracks the long-term response of properties after conversion from rainforest to agriculture (*Catena* 2020; details not retrieved) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0341816220302290)
- Casamitjana et al. (2020), *Applied Sciences* 10:5540, analysed surface soil moisture on Andosols in the Colombian Andes (Las Palmas catchment, near Medellín) under potato, bare soil and pasture using UAV multispectral imagery. They note satellite resolution is too coarse for these settings — [doi:10.3390/app10165540](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10165540)

### Inferences
- For Colombian Andisols, FC and the full retention curve should be measured on **undisturbed, field-moist cores** (never air-dried and sieved). Bulk density should be measured at the same moisture state. Legacy lab values from air-dried samples (including some IGAC and university datasets) are likely biased low, potentially by tens of percent, given the reported ~40% losses.
- Gravimetric FC values of 50%+ in Andisols translate to much lower volumetric values because ρb is <0.9 Mg m⁻³. Models (AquaCrop, DSSAT, HYDRUS) need volumetric inputs, so any gravimetric–volumetric conversion must use a ρb measured at the same moisture.
- Texture-only PTFs (Rosetta SSC, Saxton & Rawls) are structurally ill-suited to Andisols. Their key drivers (allophane, Al/Fe–humus, OC) are missing from the inputs, and particle-size analysis itself is unreliable because of poor dispersion. Extending a PTF with OC, Alo + ½Feo, or phosphate retention (as Dorel et al. did for macroporosity) is the plausible route.
- Pressure-plate FC at −33 kPa may not represent in situ FC in highly porous, freely draining Andisols. In situ drainage tests (the Ottoni Filho approach, Section 2) would help determine the right suction.

### Gaps
- Nanzyo, Shoji & Dahlgren (1993) (*Volcanic Ash Soils*, Elsevier) could not be retrieved or quoted. Its standard values (e.g., available water and ρb of Andisols) need direct citation.
- No Colombian study was found that quantifies air-dried vs. field-moist retention on the same Andisols. This appears to be a genuine gap, or a study not indexed in search.
- No Colombian Andisol-specific water-retention PTF was found. The Mexican and Ecuadorian studies are the nearest analogues.
- IGAC national-scale hydrophysical values for Andisols (e.g., in departmental soil surveys) were not retrievable.

## 2. Oxisols / Ultisols (Llanos Orientales / Altillanura, Amazon) and the FC suction question

### Takeaway
Strongly weathered, Fe/Al-oxide-rich Oxisols are microaggregated into stable "pseudo-sand" granules. They behave like clays at dry (low) matric potentials and like sands at wet (high) potentials. As a result they drain fast, and FC is reached at a much lower suction (≈ −5 to −10 kPa) than the conventional −33 kPa. Brazilian research has moved toward −6 kPa (in situ FC PTF) and −10 kPa as the standard. Using −33 kPa for Oxisols underestimates FC and AWC in a way that distorts irrigation and crop-model results.

### Cited Findings
- Tomasella & Hodnett (1998) showed that the textures of many tropical soils, "particularly oxisols such as those of Brazilian Amazonia, are outside the range of validity" of temperate PTFs. As a result, water-retention estimates "may also be significantly in error, or they may fail totally". Strongly weathered tropical soils have water retention "typical of a clay at low matric potentials and resemble sands at high matric potentials" — [Tomasella et al. 2000, SSSAJ, citing Tomasella & Hodnett 1998](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.2136/sssaj2000.641327x); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/43654057_Pedotransfer_Functions_for_the_Estimation_of_Soil_Water_Retention_in_Brazilian_Soils)
- Van den Berg, Klamt, van Reeuwijk & Sombroek (1997), "Pedotransfer functions for the estimation of moisture retention characteristics of Ferralsols and related soils", *Geoderma* 78:161–180, derived point PTFs for θ at −10 and −33 kPa and for AWC between **−10 and −1500 kPa**, plus van Genuchten parameters. A later review notes they set FC at −10 kPa rather than −33 kPa because the dataset was dominated by Ferralsols. The AWC and VG PTFs for Oxisols need Al (oxide) content as an input — [ScienceDirect, Geoderma 78](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016706197000451); [Springer chapter "PTFs for Brazilian Soils"](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-06013-2_6); [STOTEN comprehensive PTF evaluation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0048969718325488)
- Ottoni Filho et al. (2014), "Revisiting Field Capacity (FC): variation of definition of FC and its estimation from pedotransfer functions", *R. Bras. Ci. Solo* 38(6): FC is defined as the profile water distribution 48 h after the end of shallow ponded infiltration. In 22 Brazilian soils plus literature data, FC depended weakly on the amount infiltrated, antecedent moisture, morphology and groundwater level, but **strongly on basic soil properties**. θ at **−6 kPa** was the strongest predictor. A linear PTF using θ(−6 kPa) alone gave RMSR < 0.04 m³ m⁻³ for most soils and outperformed using θ at an arbitrary fixed suction — [RBCS](https://www.rbcsjournal.org/article/revisiting-field-capacity-fc-variation-of-definition-of-fc-and-its-estimation-from-pedotransfer-functions/); [SciELO](http://www.scielo.br/j/rbcs/a/smdNx6mbkGZ4JvbzBnQbnGr/?lang=en)
- Follow-ups: "An Algebraic Pedotransfer Function to Calculate Standardized in situ Determined Field Capacity", and "In situ Field Capacity in Brazilian Soils and a Derived Irrigation Management Practice Based on Water Suction" (irrigation by suction thresholds) — [ResearchGate, algebraic PTF](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/305393975_An_Algebraic_Pedotransfer_Function_to_Calculate_Standardized_in_situ_Determined_Field_Capacity); [ResearchGate, in situ FC and irrigation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358640865_In_situ_Field_Capacity_in_Brazilian_Soils_and_a_Derived_Irrigation_Management_Practice_Based_on_Water_Suction); [RBCS, ring-infiltrometer drainage FC](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbcs/a/KqZxThnbbbz9tJR6BdRxdmc/?lang=en)
- "Reexamination of the Field Capacity Concept in a Brazilian Oxisol" (de Jong van Lier & Wendroth, 2016, SSSAJ, per citing snippet) found matric potential at FC of about **−5.4 kPa**. A citing study notes that −6, −10 and −33 kPa "have been used arbitrarily" to estimate FC (citing Ottoni Filho et al. 2014) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/295243356_Reexamination_of_the_Field_Capacity_Concept_in_a_Brazilian_Oxisol)
- A Mato Grosso study comparing the flux-density method, the Arya–Paris model and a pressure chamber concluded that Oxisol FC can be obtained by subjecting undisturbed 50 cm³ samples to **10 kPa** — [Rev. Bras. Eng. Agríc. Ambiental (SciELO)](http://www.scielo.br/j/rbeaa/a/rVLNhmgQnMYMcm4FWyVxNWn/?lang=en)
- Turek, de Jong van Lier & Armindo (2020), "Estimation and mapping of field capacity in Brazilian soils", *Geoderma* 376 (ResearchGate lists 375), used SoilGrids inputs on a 0.05° (~5 km) grid. They estimated Ks and the retention curve with PTFs and simulated drainage in SWAP with flux criteria of 0.25, 1.0 and 4.0 mm d⁻¹. With the preferred 4 mm d⁻¹ criterion, FC is reached after **2 ± 1 days at −31 ± 10 cm (≈ −3 kPa)**. Fixed-suction PTF-style estimates **underestimated h_FC by twofold to almost fourfold**. Note: Reichert is not an author of this paper — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016706120301300); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342850781_Estimation_and_mapping_of_field_capacity_in_Brazilian_soils)
- The Reichert-led paper is a separate study: Reichert, Albuquerque, Solano Peraza & da Costa (2020), "Estimating water retention and availability in cultivated soils of southern Brazil", *Geoderma Regional* 21:e00277. It develops PTFs for specific tensions including FC — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352009420300262)
- Santa Catarina PTF study (RBCS): 44 profiles, FC defined at **10 kPa** and PWP at 1500 kPa — [RBCS](https://www.rbcsjournal.org/article/pedotransfer-functions-to-estimate-retention-and-availability-of-water-in-soils-of-the-state-of-santa-catarina-brazil/)
- "Field capacity of Oxisols and Quartzipsamments calculated from moisture equivalent determination" (RBCS) is another Brazilian method paper on lab FC proxies (details not retrieved) — [RBCS](https://www.rbcsjournal.org/article/field-capacity-of-oxisols-and-quartzipsamments-calculated-from-moisture-equivalent-determination/)
- Turek et al. describe the FC concept as ambiguous, with values depending on the chosen criterion (drainage rate, matric suction or drainage time) — [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342850781_Estimation_and_mapping_of_field_capacity_in_Brazilian_soils)

**Colombian Llanos / Altillanura**
- AGROSAVIA describes Altillanura soils as isohyperthermic kaolinitic Oxisols with relatively weak structure, prone to surface sealing, compaction and erosion under excessive tillage — [AGROSAVIA, "El manejo de oxisoles en las sabanas tropicales de Colombia"](https://repository.agrosavia.co/items/6101a8a9-9b32-4ab2-893d-f9866e5a4d02); [AGROSAVIA tech offer, chemical amendment of Orinoquia Oxisols](https://www.agrosavia.co/productos-y-servicios/oferta-tecnol%C3%B3gica/0124-adecuaci%C3%B3n-quimica-de-oxisoles-de-la-orinoquia)
- A 2022 *Frontiers in Environmental Science* study at AGROSAVIA's Taluma station (Puerto López, Meta; 2011–2015) measured retention curves with pressure plates and computed AWC as water held between **10 kPa and 1,500 kPa**. This shows the −10 kPa FC convention is already used by AGROSAVIA in the Altillanura — [Frontiers, doi:10.3389/fenvs.2022.687804](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2022.687804/full)
- AGROSAVIA's lab-service catalogue lists a 5-point retention curve at 0.1, 0.3, 1.0, 3.0 and 15 bar (10, 30, 100, 300, 1500 kPa). The 10 kPa point is therefore available routinely — [AGROSAVIA VIVO service listing](https://vivo.agrosavia.co/display/serana4)
- High-terrace study in Meta (Colombia): forest soils held more plant-available water than pasture and agroforestry. Available moisture was ~29.8% (agroforestry) vs. 20.2% (pasture), related to mesoporosity — [ResearchGate / Dialnet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334663925_Propiedades_quimicas_fisicas_y_biologicas_del_suelo_indicadores_del_estado_de_diferentes_ecosistemas_en_una_terraza_alta_del_departamento_del_Meta); [Dialnet PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7051757.pdf)
- A 2026 Universidad Nacional doctoral thesis monitored soil moisture for 12 months at eight sites on a transect from eastern Meta to western Vichada (3 sensors per site) and derived AWC from measured physical properties. This is a key emerging Colombian in situ dataset (not read in full) — [UNAL repository, Doctorado en Ciencias Agrarias](https://repositorio.unal.edu.co/collections/af38aa88-f50a-4238-a3e4-73e7da6d590c)

### Inferences
- For Colombian Oxisols and Ultisols (Altillanura, Piedemonte, Amazon), the Brazilian evidence supports defining FC at −6 to −10 kPa. It also supports flux-based FC (≈ −3 kPa at a 4 mm/d criterion per Turek et al.) over −33 kPa. AGROSAVIA's own Taluma work already uses −10 kPa, and its standard 5-point curve includes 10 kPa, so a −10 kPa FC is operationally feasible with existing data.
- The "pseudo-sand" behaviour means high clay content does not imply high AWC. Clay-based temperate PTFs will tend to overpredict retention at −33 and −1500 kPa relative to the actual (sand-like) drainage behaviour. This should be stated as an inference, since no direct Rosetta-on-Colombian-Oxisol test was found.
- Ultisols were not separately covered in the retrieved literature. Their behaviour likely sits between Oxisols and less weathered soils, depending on the degree of microaggregation and clay illuviation.

### Gaps
- No full text was retrievable to quote the "pseudo-sand" or microaggregation terminology from a primary source. Search snippets for van den Berg et al. (1997) did not mention microaggregation.
- No Colombian in situ FC (drainage-test) study was found for Oxisols.
- No retrievable Amazonian Colombian (Caquetá, Putumayo, Amazonas) soil-water retention data.

## 3. Performance of temperate PTFs on tropical soils, and available tropical PTFs

### Takeaway
Temperate PTFs (Rosetta, Saxton & Rawls, and others) are calibrated on soils with different mineralogy and ρb–texture relationships. They fail or degrade on Oxisols, and tropical-specific PTFs and databases perform better. The main tropical PTFs are Tomasella & Hodnett (1998), Tomasella et al. (2000, 2003), Hodnett & Tomasella (2002, VG class/continuous PTFs from the IGBP-DIS tropical subset), van den Berg et al. (1997), Minasny & Hartemink (2011), and HYBRAS (Ottoni et al. 2018). Andisols remain poorly served by all of them.

### Cited Findings
- Hodnett & Tomasella (2002), *Geoderma* ("Marked differences between van Genuchten soil water-retention parameters for temperate and tropical soils: a new water-retention pedo-transfer function developed for tropical soils"), developed class and continuous PTFs for VG parameters from the IGBP-DIS tropical subset ("IGBP/T"). A continuous PTF was derived from the whole calibration set (n = 497). These PTFs are among the few published for tropical soils. A later review judged class PTFs generally less attractive than continuous ones — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222514532_Marked_differences_between_van_Genuchten_soil_water-retention_parameters_for_temperate_and_tropical_soils_A_new_water-retention_pedo-transfer_functions_developed_for_tropical_soils); [RBCS review, humid-tropics PTFs](http://www.scielo.br/j/rbcs/a/tbj4bx7yLTZGPbwGgMtZRwj/?lang=en)
- Tomasella & Hodnett's chapter "Pedotransfer functions for tropical soils" describes testing the Hodnett & Tomasella PTF on a separate validation dataset: a calibration set of 492 curves and a validation set of 279 curves. Note the 492 vs. 497 discrepancy between sources — [ResearchGate chapter](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/251455080_Pedotransfer_functions_for_tropical_soils)
- Tomasella, Hodnett & Rossato (2000), "Pedotransfer Functions for the Estimation of Soil Water Retention in Brazilian Soils", *SSSAJ* 64:327 — [Wiley](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.2136/sssaj2000.641327x)
- Ottoni, Ottoni Filho, Schaap, Lopes-Assad & Rotunno Filho (2018), "Hydrophysical Database for Brazilian Soils (HYBRAS) and Pedotransfer Functions for Water Retention", *Vadose Zone Journal* 17:170095. The database has 445 profiles and 1075 samples. The Brazilian PTFs "performed better than the temperate models for HYBRAS", and model performance differed with clay content and mineralogy. HYBRAS mean VG parameters (1063 samples) sit relatively close to Hodnett & Tomasella (2002) values, so HYBRAS is broadly comparable to pan-tropical soils — [Wiley VZJ](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.2136/vzj2017.05.0095); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319507437_Hydrophysical_Database_for_Brazilian_Soils_HYBRAS_and_Pedotransfer_Functions_for_Water_Retention); [U. Arizona record](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/hydrophysical-database-for-brazilian-soils-hybras-and-pedotransfe/)
- HYBRAS was later combined with Embrapa Cerrados data to build VG PTFs for the Cerrado biome — [Rev. Bras. Eng. Agríc. Ambiental](http://www.scielo.br/j/rbeaa/a/mFyLYmDCYmsbTskyzbGTK3p/?lang=en); see also [PTFs for Brazilian Cerrado (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362259116_Pedotransfer_functions_for_predicting_soil-water_retention_under_Brazilian_Cerrado) and [Brazilian-savanna PTFs (academia.edu)](https://www.academia.edu/12901128/Development_of_pedotransfer_functions_for_estimating_water_retention_curve_for_tropical_soils_of_the_Brazilian_savanna)
- Minasny & Hartemink (2011), "Predicting soil properties in the tropics", *Earth-Science Reviews* 106(1–2):52–62, doi:10.1016/j.earscirev.2011.01.005, reviews PTFs for tropical soils. According to citing papers (not full text), they built PTFs for θ at −10, −33 and −1500 kPa. They considered sand, silt, clay, ρb, OC, pH, CEC and extractable Fe/Al, but settled on texture, ρb and OC. They recommend the PTF with the fewest parameters, since simple forms performed well across datasets. They also note tropical–temperate differences in ρb and CEC — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0012825211000122); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/229340398_Predicting_soil_properties_in_the_tropics)
- Botula et al. (2013), *VZJ*, applied nonparametric k-nearest-neighbour prediction of water retention for humid-tropical soils (DR Congo). The same group wrote the RBCS review "Pedotransfer functions to predict water retention for soils of the humid tropics: a review" — [Wiley VZJ](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.2136/vzj2012.0123); [RBCS review](http://www.scielo.br/j/rbcs/a/tbj4bx7yLTZGPbwGgMtZRwj/?lang=en)
- Other regional PTFs: Rhodic Ferralsols PTF (*Geoderma*); northeastern Brazil VG PTFs (RBCS); Vietnam Mekong Delta, where the SWRC of tropical soils "could be satisfactorily estimated by typical predictors" (texture, OC, ρb) — [Geoderma, rhodic ferralsols](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016706102001350); [RBCS NE Brazil](http://www.scielo.br/j/rbcs/a/Rx8kvCrgmS4kRw5qmm66cGh/?lang=en); [Mekong Delta (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/275891257_Predicting_soil_water_retention_characteristics_for_Vietnam_Mekong_Delta_soils)
- Rosetta (general): one evaluation found Rosetta adequate for θ at FC but underestimating PWP (Rubio & Llorens 2005). Another review found Rosetta accurate at FC but underestimating θs. Accuracy improves with more inputs: maximum input (PSD + ρb + θ33 + θ1500) gets closest to lab data. Site-specific PTFs beat Rosetta in a Pyrenees study. None of these are Oxisol tests — [Springer JSS, site-specific PTFs vs Rosetta](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1065/jss2008.03.281); [Schaap et al. Rosetta paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222687859_ROSETTA_A_computer_program_for_estimating_soil_hydraulic_parameters_with_hierarchical_pedotransfer_functions)
- Rosetta for Ksat in Colombia: Lozano et al. (2005), *Acta Agronómica*, tested the USDA/Rosetta hydraulic-conductivity indicator on four Valle del Cauca soils and found low correlations with measurements. They concluded it "had no application" in these soils, with variability linked mainly to structure, texture, consistency and drainage — [Acta Agronómica, UNAL](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/acta_agronomica/article/view/113)
- Costa Rica (*Agronomía Costarricense*, Dec 2025): compared four PTFs, including Rosetta, against lab θ at 33 and 1500 kPa. The empirical Peele and Briggs equations performed very poorly at FC and PWP — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398892353_Cuatro_funciones_de_pedotransferencia_para_estimar_la_retencion_de_humedad_en_suelos_Evaluacion_de_desempeno_e_implicaciones_en_el_manejo_del_recurso_hidrico)
- Venezuela (Lago de Valencia basin): −33 kPa PTFs were developed, with the caution that the regression is specific to that soil set and should not be extrapolated without validation — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279689530_FUNCIONES_DE_PEDOTRANSFERENCIA_PARA_ESTIMAR_LA_RETENCION_DE_HUMEDAD_EN_SUELOS_DE_LA_CUENCA_DEL_LAGO_DE_VALENCIA)
- Turek et al. (2020): FC estimated from fixed-suction PTFs underestimates the matric head at FC by 2× to ~4× relative to flux-based FC — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016706120301300)
- Global context: "Hydro-pedotransfer functions: a roadmap for future development" (HESS 2024) and a Global Soil Hydraulic Properties dataset (Sci. Data 2022) both address the scarcity of tropical training data — [HESS 2024](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/28/3391/2024/); [Sci. Data 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01481-5)

### Inferences
- For Colombian Oxisols, HYBRAS-based or Hodnett & Tomasella PTFs are the most defensible defaults. Rosetta and Saxton & Rawls should be treated as untested and likely biased.
- For Colombian Andisols, no PTF family (temperate or Brazilian) is calibrated on allophanic or hydric volcanic soils. HYBRAS and IGBP/T are dominated by Oxisols and Ultisols, so a Colombia-specific Andisol PTF using OC, ρb and andic indicators (Alo + ½Feo, P retention) is a clear research opportunity.
- The ρb–texture relationship differs between tropical and temperate soils (Minasny & Hartemink). PTFs should therefore include measured ρb rather than estimate it.

### Gaps
- Medrano and Teixeira Brazilian PTFs: no source found in these searches. They could not be verified and should not be cited without confirmation.
- Barros et al. (2013) (NE Brazil VG PTFs, likely the RBCS NE Brazil paper linked above): authorship not confirmed in the snippet.
- Tomasella et al. (2003) (Brazilian PTF, possibly the "Estimating soil water retention curve in rhodic ferralsols" Geoderma paper or a separate SSSAJ/Geoderma paper): not confirmed.
- Quantitative RMSE comparisons (Rosetta vs. tropical PTFs) could not be extracted without full text.
- Saxton & Rawls (2006) on tropical soils: no evaluation found.

## 4. Colombian studies, data sources and monitoring

### Takeaway
Colombian FC data are scattered across Cenicafé (coffee-zone Andisols), AGROSAVIA (Altillanura Oxisols, lab services), university theses and journals (UNAL, RFNAM, Acta Agronómica), and IGAC methods documents. No national Colombian water-retention PTF or public hydrophysical database comparable to HYBRAS was found. There is also no Colombian in situ soil-moisture network published for SMAP or SMOS validation.

### Cited Findings
- IGAC instruction "Balance Hídrico y sus Aplicaciones" (IN-AGR-PC08-01 v2): soil water storage capacity is computed from FC, PWP, bulk density and soil depth, giving the available water depth in mm. This formalizes the FC-based bucket used in IGAC agro-climatic work — [IGAC PDF](https://www.igac.gov.co/sites/default/files/listadomaestro/IN-AGR-PC08-01%20V2%20Balance%20H%C3%ADdrico%20y%20sus%20Aplicaciones.pdf)
- AGROSAVIA lab service: 5-point retention curve (0.1, 0.3, 1.0, 3.0, 15 bar) — [AGROSAVIA VIVO](https://vivo.agrosavia.co/display/serana4)
- AGROSAVIA book chapter "Balance hídrico" (Hernán Rojas P.) in the repository (content not read) — [AGROSAVIA repository](https://repository.agrosavia.co/server/api/core/bitstreams/39ea44c1-ac0c-4f0c-bc57-d4961799a74f/content)
- Cenicafé: FC at 0.33 bar and PWP at 15 bar with pressure pots and membrane. Geostatistical FC/PWP variability on Quindío Andisols (Lince-Salazar & Sadeghian 2019) — [Cenicafé 70(1)](https://publicaciones.cenicafe.org/index.php/cenicafe/article/view/3287)
- Coffee-zone hydrophysical properties vs. parent material: FC 27.1–53.8% gravimetric, with volcanic-ash soils highest — [AmeliCA](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/130/1303815003/html/)
- Taluma (Meta) AGROSAVIA study, AWC = θ(10 kPa) − θ(1500 kPa) — [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2022.687804/full)
- UNAL 2026 PhD thesis, 12-month sensor monitoring at 8 Altillanura sites (Meta–Vichada) — [UNAL repository](https://repositorio.unal.edu.co/collections/af38aa88-f50a-4238-a3e4-73e7da6d590c)
- Valle del Cauca: Rosetta-type Ksat indicator failed on four soils (Lozano et al. 2005) — [Acta Agronómica](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/acta_agronomica/article/view/113)
- Antioquia Andisols, andic criteria (RFNAM 62(1)) — [SciELO Colombia](http://www.scielo.org.co/pdf/rfnam/v62n1/a17v62n1.pdf)
- UAV-based surface moisture on Colombian Andosols (Casamitjana et al. 2020) — [Applied Sciences](https://doi.org/10.3390/app10165540)
- SMAP and SMOS validation relies on core validation sites and sparse networks, often via the International Soil Moisture Network (ISMN). No retrieved source listed a Colombian core site or network — [NASA SMAP validation report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210016750/downloads/Colliander_SMAP_VAL_R1_w_bios_211025.pdf); [RSE, SMAP core validation sites](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425717300329); [Earthdata SMAP core-site match-up data](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/catalog/nsidc-cprd-nsidc-0712-1)
- Global evaluation: SMAP captures temporal trends of ground soil moisture, with ESA CCI slightly better overall (RSE 2019) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425719302287)

### Inferences
- A Colombian hydrophysical database could be assembled from Cenicafé, AGROSAVIA lab archives (5-point curves), IGAC surveys and UNAL theses, modelled on HYBRAS. It would need metadata on sample pre-treatment (air-dried vs. field-moist) because of the Andisol drying artefact.
- The lack of a Colombian SMAP or SMOS validation network means satellite products in Colombia are effectively unvalidated locally. The coarse footprint (9–36 km) is also poorly matched to steep Andean terrain, as Casamitjana et al. note.

### Gaps
- No Colombian-developed water-retention PTF was found. Only the Ksat indicator test (Valle del Cauca) and SOC/ρb PTFs (páramo) turned up. Targeted searches in *Suelos Ecuatorianos*, *Ciencia y Tecnología Agropecuaria* and *Agronomía Colombiana* returned nothing specific, which may be an indexing limitation.
- IGAC national soil-survey hydrophysical data and its laboratory method manual (pre-treatment of samples) were not retrieved.
- No IDEAM or other Colombian soil-moisture network was found in searches. Whether any Colombian stations are in ISMN is unverified.
- CORPOICA/AGROSAVIA irrigation-scheduling studies with FC-based thresholds were not retrieved specifically, beyond the Taluma paper.

## 5. Bimodal vs. unimodal rainfall regimes and FC-based models (irrigation, AquaCrop/DSSAT in Colombia)

### Takeaway
Colombia's Andean region (and Cesar/La Guajira) has a bimodal rainfall regime with two wet and two dry seasons. The Orinoquia, Amazon, Pacific and most of the Caribbean are unimodal. In bimodal zones, FC-based bucket models are re-initialized to FC twice a year and face two short deficit windows. In unimodal zones there is one long dry season (e.g., December–April in Magdalena) with sustained depletion below FC. Colombian AquaCrop work exists (maize), but no Colombian study was found that explicitly calibrated or tested FC definitions in crop models.

### Cited Findings
- A study of rainfall seasonality in Colombia finds that the bimodal regime dominates the Andean region and Cesar/La Guajira. The unimodal regime prevails in the Pacific, Orinoquia, Amazon and Caribbean (up to Magdalena) — [ResearchGate, "Estacionalidad de la lluvia en Colombia"](https://researchgate.net/publication/309538732_Estacionalidad_de_la_lluvia_en_Colombia)
- The onset and duration of the seasons are closely linked to the passage of the ITCZ, and the pattern varies with altitude — [Eje21](https://www.eje21.com.co/2020/07/regimen-de-lluvias-unimodal-predomina-en-colombia/); [Caldas hydroclimatology, UNAL](https://bffrepositorio.unal.edu.co/server/api/core/bitstreams/5f2c14a1-6418-4092-92f7-2478d01e5320/content)
- Banana in Magdalena: December–April is the dry season with water deficit and irrigation needs. One study compared water-balance scheduling with soil-moisture (tensiometric) monitoring using a **25 kPa** threshold — [Acorbat Rev. Tecnol. Ciencia 2024](https://acorbat-rtc.com/assets/doc/7.%20Riego%20y%20Drenaje/2.%20ARTC2470.pdf)
- Water-balance irrigation scheduling starts from a soil at FC, accumulates ETc (= ETo × Kc) minus effective rain, and irrigates when depletion reaches a threshold. Interval = available water / ETc — [IGAC instruction](https://www.igac.gov.co/sites/default/files/listadomaestro/IN-AGR-PC08-01%20V2%20Balance%20H%C3%ADdrico%20y%20sus%20Aplicaciones.pdf); [SlideShare lecture (low-authority, illustrative only)](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/clase-5-balance-hdrico-y-programacin-riego/38662910)
- AquaCrop in Colombia: a UNAL study (*Agronomía Colombiana*) calibrated and validated AquaCrop for maize variety ICA V156 at Chinchiná (coffee-zone Andisols), Palmira (Valle del Cauca) and Cereté (Córdoba, Caribbean). AquaCrop simulated biomass adequately. Calibration included biomass, harvest index and yield — [Agronomía Colombiana](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/agrocol/article/view/45939)
- In AquaCrop, each soil layer is defined by, among other parameters, its upper limit of water held against gravity ("commonly called field capacity, FC"). FAO's Irrigation & Drainage Paper 66 gives guidance for parameterization, calibration and validation — [FAO I&D 66 (Spanish)](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/82bd842b-862d-4e51-8794-d80156ddab2e/content)
- Regional tropical AquaCrop examples (Cuba, Ferralítico Rojo/Oxisol-like soil): calibration focused on phenology, harvest index and depletion thresholds. One bean study used FC = 43% by volume in a Cuban clay — [Redalyc, Cuba](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/5862/586262756001/html/); [Cultivos Tropicales, Cuba](http://scielo.sld.cu/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0253-57852021000200037)

### Inferences
- In bimodal Andean zones on Andisols, the high and slowly declining moisture (RFNAM study) and two wet seasons mean profiles are often near FC. Model sensitivity to the FC value is then greatest in drainage and runoff partitioning rather than crop stress. In unimodal Llanos Oxisols, the long dry season makes the FC–PWP interval (AWC) the dominant control on stress timing. Using −33 kPa FC instead of −10 kPa would understate AWC there.
- AquaCrop and DSSAT runs in Colombia using default or texture-derived FC (from Rosetta or Saxton & Rawls via the model's soil-file tools) are likely mis-parameterized on both Andisols and Oxisols, for different reasons. Chinchiná (Andisol) and Cereté/Palmira are natural test sites for comparing FC definitions.
- Irrigation thresholds expressed in suction (e.g., 25 kPa in banana; the Brazilian suction-based in situ FC practice) are more transferable across Colombian soils than fixed volumetric FC values.

### Gaps
- No Colombian DSSAT study explicitly discussing FC or soil-parameter sensitivity was retrieved (searches focused on AquaCrop).
- No Colombian study was found that quantifies how bimodal vs. unimodal regimes interact with FC definition in model outputs.
- No IDEAM operational agro-climatic soil-water-balance product documentation was found beyond the IGAC instruction.
