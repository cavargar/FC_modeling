## 1. Richards-equation models (HYDRUS-1D, SWAP): how FC is defined implicitly, and the repo paper (Liu et al.)

### Takeaway
In Richards-equation models FC is not an input. It emerges from the van Genuchten–Mualem (VGM) retention and conductivity functions (θr, θs, α, n, Ks, l) and the boundary conditions. Studies get an "FC" back out either by reading θ at a benchmark head (−10 or −33 kPa) or by a flux-based criterion: θ when simulated drainage from an initially saturated profile falls to q_fc ≈ 0.001–0.1 mm/d (Twarakavi et al. 2009). The repo paper (actually Liu et al. **2025**, *Agronomy* 15:1973, not 2018) goes the other way. It measures FC in the field and uses it as a constraint when inverse-estimating VGM parameters in HYDRUS-1D. It reports R² of 0.74–0.88 but does not examine whether the fitted VGM curves reproduce the measured FC.

### Cited Findings

**General: FC from VGM parameters**
- Twarakavi, Sakai & Šimůnek (2009, *WRR*) define FC as "the soil water content when the drainage flux from the initially saturated soil reaches a predefined negligibly small value (q_fc)". They test q_fc = 0.001 and 0.1 mm/d (and a third value), simulating free drainage of homogeneous profiles with HYDRUS-1D (VGM). They found that reading FC off the retention curve at benchmark pressure heads "leads to inaccurate results" and derived an analytical FC equation from hydraulic parameters — [Twarakavi et al. 2009, WRR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2009WR007944); [PDF](https://www.pc-progress.com/Documents/Jirka/Twarakavi_et_al_WRR_2009_Field_Capacity.pdf)
- A 2025 coupled AquaCrop–Richards study adopted the Twarakavi criterion, estimating θ_fc per texture class "by numerically solving the van Genuchten–Mualem hydraulic equation… assuming a negligible drainage flux (q_fc) of 0.1 mm/day" — [Panthi et al., Irrigation and Drainage](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ird.70172)
- Assouline & Or (2014, *WRR*) trace the −33 kPa convention to Colman (1947) as an arbitrary value applied to all soils. They propose intrinsic static and dynamic FC criteria derived from hydraulic functions. Most of their estimated ψ_FC values are less negative (wetter) than −33 kPa — [Assouline & Or 2014, WRR](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2014WR015475)
- Other drainage-dynamics FC work in the same line: Aldrees & Ali (2019), "Capillary length and field capacity in draining soil profiles" (*WRR*); and a 2024 *Geoderma* paper on dynamic versus empirical FC estimation in fine-textured soils with a coarse interlayer, which is directly relevant to layering — [Aldrees 2019](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018WR024288); [Geoderma 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016706124002295)
- In a WOFOST (bucket) versus SWAP (Richards) benchmark, FC had to be calibrated in the bucket model to match SWAP. The calibrated FC sat **more than one pF unit closer to saturation** than common soil-based values (i.e. roughly −3 kPa or wetter rather than −10 to −33 kPa). Using common soil-based FC values underestimated available water by **up to 60%**, and **10–50% of transpired water** came from soil wetter than FC. Despite this, simulated crop production was fairly insensitive to the FC parameter — [Computers and Electronics in Agriculture 2022 (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169922001181); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358805711_Parameterizing_field_capacity_as_the_upper_limit_of_available_water_in_bucket-type_hydrological_models)

**Repo paper: `agronomy_hydrus1d_2018.md`.** Liu Q., Wang Z., Cheng L., Bai Y., Wang K., Zhang Y., "Spatiotemporal Simulation of Soil Moisture in Typical Ecosystems of Northern China: A Methodological Exploration Using HYDRUS-1D", *Agronomy* 2025, 15, 1973, doi:10.3390/agronomy15081973. The filename says 2018, but the paper was received 19 June 2025 and published 15 August 2025, and its data cover 2021–2023.
- *Site and design.* Rizhao, Shandong (coastal northern China), warm-temperate semi-humid monsoon climate. Mean annual P is 869.3 mm, with >70% falling in June–September, and land evaporation is 570 mm. Plots were a young tea plantation (*Camellia sinensis*, clonal cuttings, drip-irrigated, plastic-mulched in winter) and an adjacent grassland control, each with three replicate plots. The tea soil was excavated and backfilled in situ to 1 m in March–July 2021. The soil is a Haplic Luvisol, with tea-plot bulk density 1.55 g/cm³ and organic matter 17.93 g/kg. Insentek multi-depth capacitance probes logged hourly at 10, 20, …, 100 cm and were calibrated in situ gravimetrically — [repo file agronomy_hydrus1d_2018.md, §2.1–2.3.1](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *FC definition and measurement.* FC is defined as the water retained "after excess gravitational water has drained, and the downward movement slows significantly… typically measured 24–48 h after heavy rainfall". It was measured with the natural-rainfall method: the authors picked periods with rainfall >80 mm and averaged the daily 24-h sensor SWC once the soil "reached FC within 24–48 h" (citing Bean et al. 2018, *VZJ*, for automated FC from SWC time series) — [repo file §2.3.2](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *Measured FC.* Tea plantation FC was 29.00–40.65% vol. over 20–100 cm (minimum at 30 cm, maximum at 100 cm; 31.91% at 70 cm, 34.96% at 80 cm). Grassland FC was 22.50% (20 cm) to 28.55% (90 cm) and increased with depth. n = 6, with LSD tests showing significant between-layer differences. Tea FC was higher than grassland FC even though tea soil had lower organic matter, which the authors attribute to structure, texture and management rather than OM — [repo file §3.2, §4.1](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *Model setup.* HYDRUS-1D with the Richards equation and a single-porosity VGM model (m = 1 − 1/n, l = 0.5). Atmospheric upper boundary with surface runoff, free-drainage lower boundary. Variable time step: initial 0.001 d, minimum 1×10⁻⁵ d, maximum 5 d. ET from FAO Penman–Monteith; root water uptake as the sink term. Ten 10-cm layers to 100 cm — [repo file §2.4.1–2.4.2](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *How hydraulic parameters were obtained.* θs and bulk density were measured in the lab. θr, α, n and Ks were estimated by HYDRUS-1D inverse modelling ("Water Flow–Soil Hydraulic Parameters"). The inversion fitted daily mean observed SWC, "constrained by the measured θs", using "measured FC and soil texture analysis as constraints" (the paper does not explain how FC entered the objective function). l was fixed at 0.5, and Ks was held constant in time — [repo file Step 1, §2.4.2, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *Fitted parameters (Table 1).*
  - Tea: θr 0.057–0.105, θs 0.298–0.530, α 0.0115–0.0365 cm⁻¹, **n 1.070–1.180**, Ks 112–384 cm/d.
  - Grassland: θr 0.023–0.083, θs 0.298–0.358, α 0.0021–0.0238 cm⁻¹, n 1.231–1.785. Ks was 180–190 cm/d in the top 20 cm and only **1.5–7.1 cm/d** at 20–100 cm.
  - [repo file Table 1](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *Performance.*
  - Calibration (tea 3 Jun 2022–20 Aug 2023; grass 26 Mar 2021–20 Aug 2023): tea R² 0.806, RMSE 0.01824, MAE 0.0144; grass R² 0.809, RMSE 0.03411, MAE 0.0278.
  - Validation (tea 21 Aug–31 Dec 2023; grass 21 Aug–25 Nov 2023): tea R² 0.739, RMSE 0.0220, MAE 0.0166; grass R² 0.878, RMSE 0.0473, MAE 0.0425 (cm³/cm³).
  - Fit was weaker in the tea mid-layers (30–50 cm), which the authors attribute to winter plastic mulch suppressing water loss.
  - There were systematic biases in dry winter–spring periods and during rapid fluctuations: surface SWC was overestimated in winter–spring and underestimated in summer. The authors relate this to Kumar & Ojha (2025), who report that a constant-SHP Richards model overestimates SWC under dry conditions and underestimates it during wetting.
  - Observed versus simulated ranges at 10 cm: tea 26.51–34.82% observed vs 28.51–33.97% simulated; grass 2.09–30.58% vs 4.98–29.80%.
  - [repo file §3.3–3.4, §4.2, §5](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *The authors' FC discussion.* FC is "a critical factor for accurately determining plant-available water". FC varies with depth because of heterogeneity (citing Zettl et al. 2011 on textural layering and FC of coarse soils). Depth-specific FC supports depth-specific irrigation. The authors call for regional SM networks to estimate FC (citing Kumar et al. 2023, *AWM*, on site-specific FC and irrigation thresholds). They do not compare model-implied FC with measured FC and do not run any sensitivity analysis of FC or the VGM parameters — [repo file §4.1](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *Internal inconsistencies to note in a review.* All of these are from the repo file.
  - FC ranges differ between Results (tea 29.00–40.65%) and Conclusions (tea 29.05–40.92%).
  - The FC method requires rainfall events >80 mm, but the paper reports a maximum *daily* rainfall of 26.7 mm for 2021–2023. Multi-day totals may explain the gap, but the paper does not say so.
  - The paper describes a framework "combining a physics-based model and a data assimilation method", but no data assimilation is performed; particle filtering is listed as future work.
  - Eq. (2) duplicates Eq. (1) rather than giving the retention function.
  - The site coordinates are given as "N 119.56°, E 35.46°", with latitude and longitude swapped.
  - For the grassland, the authors say performance "improved" in validation, but RMSE rose (0.034 → 0.047) while R² rose.
  - [repo file](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)
- *VGM-implied FC from the paper's Table 1 **[computed here]**: θ(h) = θr + (θs − θr)/[1+(α|h|)ⁿ]^(1−1/n).*
  - Tea layers: θ(−10 kPa) = 0.28–0.47 and θ(−33 kPa) = 0.26–0.41. For example, at 20–30 cm θ(−33 kPa) = 0.286 against measured FC of 29.00% at 30 cm; at 90–100 cm θ(−33 kPa) = 0.412 against 40.65% measured at 100 cm. Tea θ(−1500 kPa) = 0.19–0.28, so the implied AWC (−33 to −1500 kPa) is only about 0.05–0.13, a consequence of the very low n (1.07–1.18).
  - Grassland layers: θ(−33 kPa) = 0.08–0.28 and θ(−10 kPa) = 0.15–0.32. At 10–20 cm θ(−10 kPa) = 0.207 and θ(−33 kPa) = 0.148, against measured FC of 22.5% at 20 cm. At 80–90 cm θ(−10 kPa) = 0.315 and θ(−33 kPa) = 0.283, against 28.55% at 90 cm. — computed from [repo file Table 1](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)

### Inferences
- In the repo paper, measured FC roughly matches θ at about −33 kPa in the tea soil but θ at about −10 kPa (or wetter) in the grassland topsoil. This suggests that the matric potential at "field FC" differs between land uses and layers, which is consistent with Twarakavi et al. (2009) and Assouline & Or (2014): FC is a dynamic, profile-dependent state, not a fixed-head property. The point is inferred from my calculation and has not been tested by the authors.
- The repo paper's very low tea n values (≈1.07–1.18), combined with high Ks (100–380 cm/d), describe flat retention curves with high conductivity. This parameter set probably reflects the backfilled (disturbed) soil, equifinality in the inverse problem, or both. It also implies that layer-by-layer inversion fitted to SWC time series can reproduce SWC dynamics (R² ≈ 0.8) while carrying physically questionable FC and AWC values. This is a calibration issue a literature review should flag.
- Using measured FC as an inverse-modelling constraint is a practical way to tie Richards-model parameters to an agronomic benchmark. The repo paper does not report how well the constraint was met, so it serves as a methodological example rather than as validation of FC.

### Gaps
- SWAP-specific documentation (e.g. how SWAP reports θ at user-defined heads, and its Mualem–van Genuchten/HYPRES/Staring-series parameter sources) could not be opened in this session.
- The Twarakavi et al. (2009) analytical FC equation (its exact form and q_fc sensitivity numbers) was not retrieved. Only the definition and qualitative findings are confirmed.
- The repo paper does not report how FC was weighted in the inverse objective function, the parameter uncertainties, or the correlations between parameters.

## 2. Crop models: FAO-56, AquaCrop, DSSAT, APSIM (SoilWat vs SWIM), CropSyst, STICS, WOFOST, and sensitivity to FC/DUL errors

### Takeaway
Most crop models are cascading "tipping-bucket" models. FC (DUL) is an explicit layer threshold: water above it drains at a rate set by an empirical coefficient (DSSAT/APSIM SWCON, AquaCrop τ, SWAT TT_perc), and water between FC and WP defines plant-available water (TAW). Errors in FC/DUL feed directly into TAW, stress timing and drainage/leaching. Yield is often only moderately sensitive (APSIM: a 20 mm PAWC error gives <200 kg/ha of wheat yield; WOFOST is fairly insensitive), while drainage, N leaching and ET are more sensitive.

### Cited Findings

**FAO-56**
- TAW = 1000(θFC − θWP)·Zr (mm, with Zr in m; Eq. 82). RAW = p·TAW (Eq. 83). Ks = (TAW − Dr)/((1 − p)·TAW) when Dr > RAW (Eq. 84) — [FAO-56 Ch. 8 "Soil water availability"](https://www.fao.org/3/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm); [pyfao56 v1.1 docs (USDA-ARS)](https://ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/57795/Brekel2023%20-%20pyfao56%20v1.1.pdf); [swb docs](https://swb.readthedocs.io/en/latest/swb.html)
- p normally ranges from 0.30 (shallow-rooted crops, ETc > 8 mm/d) to 0.70 (deep-rooted crops, ETc < 3 mm/d), and 0.50 is common. It is adjusted as p = p_Table22 + 0.04(5 − ETc), bounded to 0.1 ≤ p ≤ 0.8 — [FAO-56 Ch. 8](https://www.fao.org/4/X0490e/x0490e0e.htm)
- In the FAO-56 water balance, deep percolation occurs when the root zone exceeds FC (Dr < 0 is reset to 0, with the excess as DP), so FC is a hard cap on daily-step storage — [FAO-56 Ch. 8](https://www.fao.org/3/x0490e/x0490e0e.htm) **[the DP rule is from FAO-56 Ch. 8 equations; page not opened in-session]**

**AquaCrop**
- Soil inputs are θSAT, θFC, θWP, Ksat and the drainage coefficient τ. τ governs the drainage rate between saturation and FC, with lower τ meaning slower drainage. FAO training material gives example values of about 0.2 for clay and 0.9 for sand, derived from Ksat — [FAO AquaCrop Reference Manual Ch. 2 (2009)](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/faowater/docs/AquaCropChapter2.pdf); [FAO webinar "Simulation of soil water and salt balance" (Nov 2024)](https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/GSP/INSAS/wEBINAR_26Nov24/2._SimulationOfSoilWaterAndSaltBalance.pdf)
- The drainage function in the AquaCrop calculation-procedures chapter is Δθ/Δt = τ(θSAT − θFC)·[exp(θ − θFC) − 1]/[exp(θSAT − θFC) − 1] for θ > θFC (zero at or below FC), with τ = 0.0866·Ksat^0.35 (Ksat in mm/d, 0 ≤ τ ≤ 1). FC is therefore the level at which drainage ceases in each compartment — [AquaCrop v7.x Ch. 3, Calculation procedures](https://www.fao.org/docs/devbuildingforwardbetterlibraries/default-document-library/chapter3aquacropv73.pdf) **[equation unverified in-session: PDF could not be fetched; verify exact form against the manual]**
- AquaCrop's cascading scheme is criticized for omitting capillarity and redistribution below FC. Panthi et al. (2025) coupled AquaCrop with a Richards solver for this reason and set FC with the q_fc = 0.1 mm/d criterion — [Panthi et al. 2025, Irrigation and Drainage](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ird.70172)

**DSSAT (CERES/CROPGRO)**
- DSSAT soil water uses a one-dimensional tipping-bucket approach for layered soils. Water above DUL drains at a rate set by a single, profile-wide drainage coefficient SWCON (0–1, d⁻¹ fraction), which reflects the most impeding layer. Assigning one value to the whole profile has been called theoretically unsupported — [Shelia et al. 2018, J. Hydrol. Hydromech.](https://www.pc-progress.com/Documents/Jirka/JHH/Shelia_et_al_JHH_2018.pdf); [DSSAT.net soil water](https://dssat.net/soil-water/); [Modifications to the DSSAT vertical drainage model (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232108129_Modifications_to_the_Dssat_Vertical_Drainage_Model_for_More_Accurate_Soil_Water_Dynamics_Estimation)
- SWCON values by drainage class, as used in a Spanish CERES-Wheat/Maize calibration: about **0.8** (excessively drained), **0.6** (well drained), **0.4** (moderately well drained), down to **0.005** (very poorly drained) — [UNFCCC/Guereña et al., DSSAT calibration in Spain](https://unfccc.int/files/national_reports/non-annex_i_natcom/cge/application/pdf/agriculture.dssatvalidation.pdf)
- DSSAT v4.6+ includes pedotransfer functions that estimate LL, DUL and SAT from sand, silt, clay, SOC and bulk density — [Frontiers Environ. Sci. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2019.00013/full)
- In a CROPGRO-Canola sensitivity analysis, DUL, LL and the drainage rate were among the most sensitive parameters for flowering and maturity dates, yield, biomass and maximum LAI. In another sensitivity study, SWCON ranked among the most influential parameters for leached nitrate-N (sensitivity index ≥ 0.02) — [Frontiers Environ. Sci. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2019.00013/full) (reported via search extract; specific model/crop attribution should be checked)

**APSIM: SoilWat (bucket) vs SWIM (Richards)**
- In SoilWat, SWCON is "the proportion of soil water above the drained upper limit (DUL) that drains in one day". Water between DUL and SAT can leave a layer as saturated flow, and unsaturated flow below DUL is handled by a diffusivity function — [APSIM SoilWat documentation](https://www.apsim.info/documentation/model-documentation/soil-modules-documentation/soilwat/)
- SWCON is SoilWat-specific, and K_DUL is the SWIM3 analogue. In the S-map/APSIM parameter work, SWCON values were derived by matching SWIM3 drainage rates, i.e. the bucket coefficient was calibrated against Richards-model output — [S-map parameters for APSIM (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358399559_S-map_parameters_for_APSIM); [APSIM SWIM3 docs](https://www.apsim.info/documentation/model-documentation/soil-modules-documentation/swim3/)
- SoilWat solves processes sequentially on a daily step, which makes post-rain/irrigation redistribution hard to capture. SWIM solves them simultaneously; a 2026 APSIM–HYDRUS coupling study was motivated by this — [Agricultural Systems 2026 (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308521X26002155)
- Chapagain et al. (2023, *Front. Agron.*) built 8 APSIM model structures (2 crop × 2 soil water [SoilWat/SWIM3] × 2 irrigation models) on Ferralsols, Alisols and Chernozems. Crop-model choice was the dominant structural uncertainty (**>60%** of variance) for all soils, including for drainage and N leaching. Irrigation SDs were 11–33.3 mm against a mean of about 500 mm, and the irrigation model dominated irrigation demand. The soil water model was therefore a secondary source of structural uncertainty — [Chapagain et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2023.1213074/full)
- In Vogeler et al. (2022), varying the SWIM3 hydraulic parameters affected soil moisture status but had generally little effect on drainage, N leaching and pasture production. SWIM3 overestimated soil evaporation right after rain, while SoilWat predicted evaporation better — [Sensitivity analysis of soil parameters in APSIM (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363685765_Sensitivity_analysis_of_soil_parameters_in_the_Agricultural_Production_Systems_sIMulator_APSIM); [APSIM SWIM validation](https://docs.apsim.info/validation/SWIM)
- A related *J. Hydrol.* (2021) paper asks how important the unsaturated K description is for simulated saturation, drainage and pasture yield — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169421003048)
- APSIM PAWC sensitivity: PAWC errors of up to **20 mm** changed water-limited wheat yield potential by **<200 kg/ha**. DUL mattered mainly through the resulting PAWC rather than independently — [Field Crops Research, "How well do we need to estimate plant-available water capacity…" (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037837741831432X)
- There is a protocol for building APSIM soil descriptions (DUL/LL15/SAT/SWCON) — [MethodsX 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2215016121003563)
- A Nigerian sorghum APSIM evaluation found deep drainage to be a weak component — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/291303839_Evaluation_of_APSIM_to_simulate_soil_water_balance_of_a_long_term_Sorghum_based_cropping_system_in_Sudano-Sahelian_zone_of_Nigeria)

**CropSyst, STICS, WOFOST**
- CropSyst's soil editor manual documents the hydraulic properties (FC, PWP, Ksat, air-entry potential, Campbell b). CropSyst offers a cascade (bucket) option and a finite-difference (Richards-type) option — [CropSyst soil hydraulics manual](https://modeling.bsyse.wsu.edu/CS_Suite/cropsyst/soil_editor/manual/hydraulics.htm) **[the two-option detail is unverified in-session]**
- STICS sensitivity analysis (Ruget et al. 2002, *Agronomie*): FC was among the parameters with a systematic effect. Soil-dependent modules influenced drainage and leaching far more than yield, which was driven mainly by shoot-production modules — [Agronomie 2002](https://www.agronomy-journal.org/articles/agro/abs/2002/02/04/04.html)
- WOFOST (bucket): the optimal FC depends on target variable, soil type, climate and crop drought sensitivity. Common soil-based FC values underestimated available water by up to 60%, yet crop production was fairly insensitive — [Comput. Electron. Agric. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169922001181)
- In a North China soil–crop multi-model comparison, CropSyst underestimated yields while STICS and WOFOST overestimated them — [Sci. Rep. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep25755)
- An exploratory sensitivity analysis compares CropSyst, WARM and WOFOST — [Italian J. Agrometeorology](https://www.agrometeorologia.it/wp-content/uploads/2010/11/2.pdf) (contents not retrieved)

### Inferences
- In bucket crop models, FC plays two roles at once: it is the drainage threshold and the upper bound of available water. An FC error therefore moves TAW, stress onset and percolation together. Richards models separate these roles, so "FC error" there shows up as errors in α, n and Ks.
- The evidence suggests an asymmetry. Yield is buffered against moderate FC/DUL errors (APSIM <200 kg/ha for 20 mm PAWC; WOFOST insensitive; STICS yield driven by shoot modules), whereas drainage, N leaching and irrigation scheduling are more sensitive. A review should keep "yield sensitivity" and "water/N balance sensitivity" separate.
- SWCON and τ are effectively calibration knobs that absorb the mismatch between a fixed-threshold FC and real gradual drainage. Deriving SWCON from SWIM3 runs (S-map) acknowledges this.

### Gaps
- The exact AquaCrop drainage equation and τ–Ksat relation, the APSIM SoilWat equations and DSSAT's default SWCON lookup (e.g. Ritchie's range by drainage class) could not be verified from primary PDFs in this session.
- No quantitative WOFOST/STICS/CropSyst DUL-error → yield elasticities were found beyond the qualitative statements above.
- No crop-model study was found that perturbs FC alone across a multi-model ensemble (e.g. within AgMIP) and reports yield or drainage spread.

## 3. Hydrologic and land-surface models: SWAT, VIC, Noah/Noah-MP, CLM, JULES

### Takeaway
Hydrologic and land-surface models (LSMs) use FC in two main ways. In SWAT it is a percolation threshold: only water above FC (−33 kPa) percolates, routed with TT_perc = (SAT − FC)/Ksat. In VIC, Noah/Noah-MP and JULES it is a reference "critical" moisture for ET/transpiration stress (VIC Wcr ≈ 70% of FC; Noah SMCREF; JULES θcrit). The values usually come from texture-class lookup tables that trace to Cosby et al. (1984) PTFs (Clapp–Hornberger/Campbell parameters), with FC and WP added afterwards (Chen & Dudhia 2001). These defaults matter: in a Texas Noah-MP study, FC was the parameter with the largest influence on ET, and underestimated FC led to underestimated soil moisture.

### Cited Findings
- **SWAT.** AWC is the water between FC (−0.033 MPa) and PWP (−1.5 MPa), with AWC = FC − WP entered per layer in the .sol file. Water percolates only when layer SW exceeds FC and the layer below is not saturated. Drainable water is the excess above FC, routed as w_perc = SW_excess·[1 − exp(−Δt/TT_perc)], with travel time TT_perc = (SAT − FC)/Ksat (hours) — [SWAT 2009 Theory](https://swat.tamu.edu/media/99192/swat2009-theory.pdf); [SWAT input .SOL ch. 22](https://swat.tamu.edu/media/69365/ch22_input_sol.pdf); [SWAT+ percolation docs](https://swatplus.gitbook.io/io-docs/theoretical-documentation/section-2-hydrology/chapter-2-3-soil-water/2-3.2-percolation); [SWAT+ awc](https://swatplus.gitbook.io/io-docs/introduction-1/soils/soils.sol/awc); [SWAT+ hydrology](https://swatplus.com/t3m4Docs/model-theory/hydrology/)
- **VIC.** Wcr_FRACT is the critical-point moisture as a fraction of maximum moisture (porosity × depth). Below it, transpiration is reduced. It is set at about 70% of FC, with FC defined at −33 kPa (1/3 bar) and WP at 15 bar. The default texture-class FC/WP values come from Schaake's ARS texture-class averages and are converted to fractions of porosity — [VIC parameter definitions (GitHub)](https://github.com/UW-Hydro/VIC/blob/master/docs/Documentation/Definitions.md); [VIC soil texture docs](https://vic.readthedocs.io/en/master/Documentation/soiltext/); [VIC Params](https://vic.readthedocs.io/en/master/Documentation/Drivers/Image/Params/)
- **Noah / Noah-MP.**
  - SMCREF (REFSMC) is the "reference soil moisture (field capacity)", where transpiration begins to be stressed. The soil factor scales between θwilt and θref.
  - Cosby et al. (1984) published only BB, MAXSMC, SATPSI and SATDK for 11 of the 12 USDA textures (no silt). Chen & Dudhia (2001) later added θref and θwilt.
  - REFSMC for silt in later tables differs from the Chen & Dudhia (2001) values, so the table version matters.
  - [Noah-MP technote v0.2](https://www.jsg.utexas.edu/noah-mp/files/Noah-MP_Technote_v0.2.pdf); [Noah-MP source module_sf_noahmplsm.F90](https://www.nco.ncep.noaa.gov/pmb/codes//nwprod/hafs.v2.1.5/sorc/hafs_forecast.fd/NOAHMP-interface/noahmp/src/module_sf_noahmplsm.F90); [Chen & Dudhia 2001, MWR](https://www2.mmm.ucar.edu/people/dudhia/files/papers/chendudhia101.pdf); [Noah SOILPARM](https://ral.ucar.edu/sites/default/files/public/product-tool/noah-multiparameterization-land-surface-model-noah-mp-lsm/soilparm.html); [Kishné et al. 2017, Geoderma](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016706116304888)
- Yimam et al. (2025) replaced Noah-MP default soil tables with NCSS-measured data (6,749 samples, 850 profiles, Texas): direct air-dry/PWP/FC water contents, with porosity from bulk density following Cosby. **FC influenced ET estimates the most** among the soil parameters — [Yimam et al. 2025, Agrosyst. Geosci. Environ.](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/agg2.70048)
- In a study implementing global soil databases in Noah-MP (HESS 2025), sites where FC was significantly underestimated consistently had underestimated soil moisture — [HESS 29, 2551, 2025](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/29/2551/2025/)
- Noah-MP plant-hydraulics development (Li et al. 2021, *JAMES*) set reference FC directly per site from Saxton et al. (1986) rather than from the default table — [Li et al. 2021](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2020MS002214)
- Noah surface drying rates versus SMAP: model drying behaviour depends on the soil hydraulic parameters — [HESS 22, 1649, 2018](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/22/1649/2018/)
- **JULES.** β is computed per layer between θwilt and θcrit (critical point, treated as FC), weighted by root fraction. A parameter p0 shifts the onset of stress: β = 1 above θwilt + (θcrit − θwilt)(1 − p0), and p0 = 0.4 has been used to delay stress — [ResearchGate figure source on JULES β](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/JULES-soil-moisture-stress-factor-b-with-p00-solid-line-and-p003-dashed-line-The_fig1_334672145)

### Inferences
- In LSMs, FC mostly controls *when ET becomes water-limited* rather than drainage, because drainage is computed with Richards-type or Darcy fluxes using Clapp–Hornberger/Cosby K(θ). FC errors therefore propagate mainly into latent heat and soil moisture memory, consistent with Yimam et al. (2025) and HESS (2025).
- Because the LSM FC/WP tables are derived after the fact and are texture-class-based, they cannot represent the land-use or depth contrasts in FC measured in the repo paper (e.g. tea 29–41% vs grass 22.5–28.6% vol. on the same soil type).

### Gaps
- No primary CLM5 documentation was retrieved. CLM uses Clapp–Hornberger/Cosby parameters, with soil-water stress based on matric potential between "open" and "closed" stomatal potentials rather than an explicit FC, but this was **not verified in-session**.
- The JULES default θcrit definition (θ at −33 kPa from Brooks–Corey or van Genuchten parameters) is likewise unverified.
- The Noah REFSMC formula (often described as θ at which K reaches a small threshold flux) could not be confirmed. One extract mentions a K = 0.1 mm/d criterion in Kishné et al. (2017), while the value of about 0.75 mm/d sometimes quoted could not be verified.

## 4. Tipping-bucket vs Richards comparisons: drainage, post-rain water content, layered-soil FC overestimation

### Takeaway
Under free drainage, buckets and Richards solvers often give similar seasonal water contents and yields. For example, DSSAT bucket vs coupled DSSAT–HYDRUS gives an index of agreement d of 0.9 vs 0.8 for θ and 0.94–0.99 for pod yield, and the EPIC bucket and Richards options gave similar θ. They diverge with shallow water tables (no capillary rise in buckets), at high water contents (buckets drain too fast), during post-rain redistribution, and in layered soils. In layered soils, a bucket FC taken from the retention curve misrepresents the perched or retarded drainage above texture contrasts, and the effective FC is usually wetter than the −33 kPa value.

### Cited Findings
- Shelia, Šimůnek, Boote & Hoogenboom (2018) coupled HYDRUS-1D into DSSAT and compared it with the tipping bucket using DSSAT-distributed datasets. The index of agreement d for volumetric water content was 0.9 (original) vs 0.8 (coupled). For pod mass (4 soybean + 4 peanut treatments) both gave d = 0.94–0.99 — [Shelia et al. 2018, JHH 66(2):232–245](https://www.pc-progress.com/Documents/Jirka/JHH/Shelia_et_al_JHH_2018.pdf); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/66x523hz)
- Faria & Bowen (2003) found that adding a Darcy–Buckingham steady-state flow approach to DSSAT significantly improved performance over the tipping bucket — as cited in [Shelia et al. 2018](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323084806_Coupling_DSSAT_and_HYDRUS-1D_for_simulations_of_soil_water_dynamics_in_the_soil-plant-atmosphere_system)
- Longo et al. (2021, *VZJ*) tested EPIC's Richards submodel against its tipping bucket over 9 years. Both predicted similar θ under free drainage, while Richards predicted θ and ET better under a shallow water table. Both overestimated percolation under some conditions. Tipping buckets perform poorly at high θ because they overestimate rapid drainage and omit upward flux. Denitrification was mischaracterized because simulated SWC rarely exceeded FC despite rapid drainage — [Longo et al. 2021, VZJ](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/vzj2.20142); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353227446_Testing_the_EPIC_Richards_submodel_for_simulating_soil_water_dynamics_under_different_bottom_boundary_conditions)
- Emerman (1995, *J. Hydrol.* 171:23–47) argued that "the tipping bucket model is essentially a model for macropore flow". A hybrid of tipping bucket (macropores) and Richards (micropores) fitted measured water contents equally well under reasonable θs assumptions — [J. Hydrol. 1995](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022169495027358)
- In the WOFOST bucket vs SWAP Richards benchmark, matching the bucket to SWAP required an FC more than one pF unit wetter than common values. Common FC values underestimated available water by up to 60% — [Comput. Electron. Agric. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169922001181)
- Bucket-model parameterization for SVAT/hydrological use is discussed in HESS 15:3877 (2011) — [HESS 2011](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/15/3877/2011/hess-15-3877-2011.pdf)
- Layering: Zettl et al. (2011) analysed the influence of textural layering on the FC of coarse soils. A 2024 *Geoderma* study compared dynamic (drainage-based) and empirical FC estimation in fine-textured soils with a coarse interlayer. Both show that profile layering changes the effective FC relative to single-horizon retention values — [Zettl et al. 2011, Can. J. Soil Sci.](https://doi.org/10.4141/cjss09117) (cited in repo paper ref. 49); [Geoderma 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016706124002295)
- AquaCrop–Richards coupling (Panthi et al. 2025) was motivated by the lack of capillarity and sub-FC redistribution in AquaCrop's cascade, which limits accuracy in textured or layered soils — [Panthi et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ird.70172)
- An arXiv tomato-field study compared soil-crop system models against proximal gamma-ray SWC — [arXiv 1805.02520](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.02520) (details not retrieved)

### Inferences
- "FC overestimation" and "FC underestimation" both appear in this literature, depending on the reference. Relative to what a Richards model or a field drainage test implies, a −33 kPa FC is often *too dry* for coarse or layered soils (WOFOST/SWAP: >1 pF wetter; Assouline & Or: ψ_FC mostly > −33 kPa). Above a coarse interlayer, a single-horizon FC can mismatch in either direction. A review should therefore define the reference explicitly.
- Tipping buckets push the profile to FC within a day or so, and they cannot hold water above FC in a fine layer sitting on a coarse one (capillary barrier). Richards models reproduce that retarded drainage. This is probably why the repo paper's measured, depth-varying FC differs from texture-based values.

### Gaps
- No study was found that quantified post-rain θ differences in mm or vol.% between a bucket crop model and Richards specifically in a layered profile. The quantitative numbers in Longo et al. (2021) and Shelia et al. (2018) beyond d values were not retrieved.
- The magnitude of the "FC overestimation in layered soils" problem (e.g. vol.% bias of a −33 kPa FC above a coarse layer) was not obtained in numbers.

## 5. Uncertainty contributed by FC parameterization (ensembles, sensitivity analyses)

### Takeaway
FC/DUL uncertainty contributes moderate uncertainty to yield and larger uncertainty to water- and N-balance fluxes and ET. Structural choices (crop model, irrigation module) generally outweigh soil-water-module choice in APSIM ensembles. In gridded global ensembles, though, soil hydrology parameterization strongly shifts yield levels. In LSMs, FC is among the most influential soil parameters for ET.

### Cited Findings
- APSIM: a ±20 mm PAWC error changed water-limited wheat yield potential by <200 kg/ha — [Field Crops Res. (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S037837741831432X)
- APSIM structural ensemble: crop model >60% of variance. SoilWat vs SWIM3 was a secondary source of uncertainty for yield, drainage and leaching. Irrigation SDs were 11–33 mm on a mean of about 500 mm — [Chapagain et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/agronomy/articles/10.3389/fagro.2023.1213074/full); see also [Investigating the effects of APSIM model configuration on outputs](https://www.jojeda.com/publication/apsim-model-outputs/)
- APSIM-SWIM3 hydraulic-parameter sensitivity: large effects on soil moisture but generally small effects on drainage, N leaching and pasture yield in the tested system — [Vogeler et al. 2022 (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363685765_Sensitivity_analysis_of_soil_parameters_in_the_Agricultural_Production_Systems_sIMulator_APSIM)
- Global gridded crop model ensemble (EPIC-based, harmonized management): yield levels were highly sensitive to soil parameterization, particularly hydrology. Between-model structural differences outweighed within-model configuration differences — [Folberth et al. 2019, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6746385/)
- STICS: FC has a systematic effect, stronger on drainage and leaching than on yield — [Ruget et al. 2002](https://www.agronomy-journal.org/articles/agro/abs/2002/02/04/04.html)
- DSSAT: DUL, LL and the drainage rate were among the most sensitive inputs for phenology, yield, biomass and LAI (CROPGRO-Canola). SWCON was among the most influential for nitrate leaching — [Frontiers Environ. Sci. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2019.00013/full)
- Noah-MP: FC was the most influential soil parameter for ET in Texas, and FC underestimation produced systematic soil-moisture underestimation in a global-soil-database implementation — [Yimam et al. 2025](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/agg2.70048); [HESS 2025](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/29/2551/2025/)
- Model-based irrigation under uncertainty: soil-parameter uncertainty affects the performance of irrigation strategies — [PMC7562963](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7562963/) (details not retrieved)
- WOFOST: FC calibration is target-variable-dependent, so a single "best" FC does not exist across outputs — [Comput. Electron. Agric. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0168169922001181)
- Repo paper: no uncertainty or sensitivity analysis was done. The authors acknowledge that a constant Ks and constant SHPs may cause systematic dry-season and wetting biases, and they propose particle-filter data assimilation as future work — [repo file agronomy_hydrus1d_2018.md, §4.2, §5](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy15081973)

### Inferences
- The uncertainty FC introduces depends on the output. For yield in rainfed temperate systems, FC/DUL errors of ±0.02–0.03 m³/m³ (about ±20 mm PAWC over 1 m) appear second-order. For drainage, N leaching, irrigation-trigger timing and LSM latent heat, FC is often a first-order parameter. The ±0.02–0.03 m³/m³ figure is my own conversion of the APSIM 20 mm figure over a 1 m profile.
- Because SWCON and τ compensate for FC error during calibration, FC and drainage-coefficient uncertainties are correlated. One-at-a-time sensitivity analyses probably understate their joint effect.

### Gaps
- No global variance-based (Sobol) study was found that isolates the FC/DUL contribution to yield variance across multiple crop models.
- No quantitative ensemble spread attributable specifically to PTF choice for FC in LSMs (Cosby vs Saxton vs Rosetta-based) was retrieved in this session.
